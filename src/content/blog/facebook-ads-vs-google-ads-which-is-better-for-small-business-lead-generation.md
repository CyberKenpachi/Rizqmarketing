Small businesses often have limited advertising budgets, so choosing the right platform matters. You want your ads to reach potential customers, generate inquiries, and create opportunities to grow your business. But with several advertising options available, deciding where to invest can be challenging.

Two of the most popular choices are Facebook Ads and Google Ads. Both can help businesses generate leads, but they work in different ways.

Google Ads can connect your business with people actively searching for products or services. Facebook Ads, now part of Meta Ads, can introduce your business to people based on their interests, behaviors, and other audience signals while they browse Facebook and Instagram.

So, which platform is better for small business lead generation?

At **[Rizq Marketing](https://rizqmarketing.com/)**, we look beyond the number of clicks or impressions when evaluating paid advertising. The right platform should match your business goals, customer behavior, budget, and ability to turn inquiries into customers.

Let's compare the two and explore when each one makes sense.



## **1. How Facebook Ads and Google Ads Work**

Before choosing a platform, it helps to understand how each one reaches potential customers.

### **Facebook Ads: Reaching People Based on Audience Signals**

Facebook Ads allow businesses to reach people while they browse Facebook and Instagram. Instead of waiting for someone to search for a specific service, you can introduce your offer to an audience that may be interested in what you provide.

**You can use different creative formats, including:**

* Image advertisements
* Short-form videos
* Carousel advertisements
* Lead generation forms
* Messenger and other messaging placements

For example, a local fitness studio can use Facebook and Instagram ads to promote a membership offer to people in its service area. A home improvement company can show before-and-after project images to introduce its services to potential customers.

The advantage is the ability to create awareness and interest before someone actively starts looking for a provider.

### **Google Ads: Reaching People Who Are Already Searching**

Google Ads works differently, particularly with Search campaigns. Your ads can appear when people search for products or services related to your business.

For example, someone searching for "emergency plumber near me" may be actively looking for help. A plumbing company running a relevant Google Search campaign can appear when that potential customer needs the service.

Google Ads can also include other campaign types across Google's advertising channels, such as YouTube, Display, and Performance Max. These formats can support awareness, consideration, and lead generation beyond traditional search results.

The main advantage of Search advertising is that it can connect your business with people who have already expressed interest through their searches.



## 2. Facebook Ads vs. Google Ads: Key Differences

Both Facebook Ads and Google Ads offer opportunities to generate leads, but they reach potential customers in different ways. Understanding these differences can help you choose the platform that best fits your small business goals.

### How People Discover Your Business

* **Facebook Ads (Meta Ads):** Your advertisements appear while people browse Facebook and Instagram. You can reach audiences based on demographics, interests, behaviors, and other audience signals.
* **Google Ads:** Your advertisements can appear when people search for products or services related to your business. This allows you to connect with potential customers who may already be looking for a solution.

### Targeting Options

* **Facebook Ads:** Focus on audience targeting, including demographics, interests, behaviors, and campaign optimization.
* **Google Ads:** Focus on keywords, search intent, location targeting, and campaign settings to reach people searching for relevant products or services.

### Creative Requirements

* **Facebook Ads:** Effective campaigns often use engaging images, videos, carousel formats, compelling headlines, and clear offers.
* **Google Ads:** Search campaigns rely on relevant ad copy, carefully selected keywords, advertising assets, and landing pages that match the searcher's needs.

### Lead Generation Formats

* **Facebook Ads:** Businesses can generate inquiries through instant lead forms, website forms, calls, and messaging placements.
* **Google Ads:** Businesses can generate leads through website forms, phone calls, and available lead form assets.

### Main Advertising Strength

* **Facebook Ads:** Particularly useful for building brand awareness, introducing new offers, and generating interest among potential customers.
* **Google Ads:** Particularly useful for capturing existing demand and reaching people actively searching for specific products or services.

### Important Factors to Consider

* **Facebook Ads:** Creative quality, audience relevance, offer presentation, and lead qualification can significantly affect campaign performance.
* **Google Ads:** Search intent, keyword relevance, competition, landing page quality, and conversion tracking play important roles.

Neither platform guarantees better leads or lower advertising costs. Results depend on your industry, target audience, offer, campaign setup, competition, and follow-up process.

The best platform is the one that helps your business attract relevant prospects and turn those inquiries into paying customers.



## **3. When Facebook Ads Are Better for Small Businesses**

Facebook Ads can be a strong option when your business needs to introduce an offer, build awareness, or reach potential customers who may not be actively searching for your services.

They are particularly useful for businesses that can communicate their value through visual content.

### **Businesses With Visual Products or Services**

If customers can understand your offer by seeing it, Facebook and Instagram can provide useful advertising opportunities.

**Examples include:**

* Restaurants promoting new menu items
* Beauty businesses showcasing treatments
* Real estate agents presenting properties
* Fitness studios promoting memberships
* Home improvement companies showing completed projects
* Event businesses advertising upcoming services

Photos and videos can help people understand what your business offers before they decide to inquire.

### **Businesses Introducing New Offers**

Google Search is useful when people already know what they want. But what happens when customers are not familiar with your product or service?

Facebook Ads can help introduce a new offer, promote a seasonal service, or encourage people to learn more about your business.

For example, a new local business can use social advertising to introduce its services to people in its target market and build recognition over time.

### **Businesses That Benefit From Audience Retargeting**

Someone may visit your website, watch a video, or interact with your social media content without contacting your business immediately.

With appropriate tracking and audience settings, Meta campaigns can help reconnect with eligible people who previously interacted with your business.

This gives you another opportunity to communicate your offer and encourage the next step.



## **4. When Google Ads Are Better for Small Businesses**

Google Ads can be a better starting point when people are already searching for the products or services you provide.

This is particularly important for businesses that solve immediate problems or offer services customers actively seek.

### **Service Businesses With High-Intent Searches**

**Consider businesses such as:**

* Plumbers
* Roofers
* HVAC contractors
* Electricians
* Lawyers
* Accountants
* Pest control companies
* Digital marketing agencies

People searching for these services may already have a specific need. A relevant Google Search campaign can help the business appear when those potential customers are looking for a provider.

### **Businesses Targeting Local Customers**

Google Search campaigns can be configured around specific service areas and relevant search terms.

For example, a roofing company serving Miami may target searches related to roof repair, roof replacement, and roofing contractors in its service area.

This helps connect advertising with the locations and services that matter to the business.

### **Businesses With Clear Conversion Goals**

**Google Ads can be useful when the desired action is straightforward, such as:**

* Requesting a quote
* Booking a consultation
* Calling a business
* Scheduling an appointment
* Submitting a service inquiry

The campaign can be measured against these actions rather than relying only on website visits or ad clicks.

Google's[ **guide to generating high-quality leads**](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13489421) also emphasizes choosing conversion goals that reflect meaningful business outcomes.



## **5. Which Platform Generates Better-Quality Leads?**

This is one of the most important questions for small businesses.

A platform may generate many inquiries, but that does not necessarily mean those inquiries will become customers.

For example, a Facebook lead form may make it easy for someone to submit their contact information. However, some people may complete the form because they are casually interested rather than ready to purchase.

A Google Search lead may come from someone actively looking for a service, but that person may still be comparing several providers or checking prices.

The source alone does not determine lead quality.

**We recommend evaluating leads based on:**

* **Relevance:** Does the person need the service or product you offer?
* **Location:** Is the inquiry from an area your business serves?
* **Intent:** Is the person ready to purchase, or are they only researching?
* **Budget:** Can the potential customer afford the service?
* **Conversion potential:** Does the inquiry have a realistic chance of becoming a sale?

Businesses should also track what happens after a lead is submitted. Recording which inquiries are contacted, qualified, booked, and converted helps identify which campaigns are producing valuable opportunities.



## **6. How Much Should a Small Business Spend on Facebook Ads or Google Ads?**

There is no universal advertising budget that works for every small business.

Google Ads costs can vary based on keyword competition, industry, location, and the quality of the campaign. Facebook Ads costs depend on factors such as audience size, creative performance, campaign objective, competition, and delivery.

Rather than choosing a budget based on what another business spends, start with your own customer acquisition goals.

**Consider these questions:**

* How much revenue does an average new customer generate?
* How much can the business afford to spend to acquire a customer?
* What percentage of inquiries typically become paying customers?
* How many qualified leads can the sales team handle?
* How much demand exists for the service or product?

For example, if a business can only handle a limited number of new inquiries each week, generating a large volume of low-quality leads may not be helpful.

We recommend starting with a realistic test budget, measuring meaningful results, and adjusting spending based on campaign performance.



## **7. Can Small Businesses Use Facebook Ads and Google Ads Together?**

Yes. In fact, using both platforms can create a more complete advertising strategy when the business has enough budget and resources to manage them properly.

Each platform can serve a different purpose.

Google Ads can capture demand from people actively searching for a solution. Facebook and Instagram Ads can introduce the business to potential customers, promote offers, and support retargeting.

**For example, a local home renovation company could:**

1. Use Google Search campaigns to reach people searching for renovation contractors.
2. Use Facebook and Instagram ads to showcase completed projects and customer testimonials.
3. Retarget eligible website visitors and social media engagers with relevant offers.
4. Track inquiries and identify which campaigns generate qualified opportunities.

The goal is not to run both platforms simply because they are available. Each campaign should have a defined role in the customer journey.

If the advertising budget is limited, it may be more practical to start with one platform, establish what works, and expand once the business has reliable performance data.



## **8. Common Advertising Mistakes That Can Waste Your Budget**

Choosing the right platform is only one part of lead generation. Poor campaign management can affect results on either platform.

Here are some issues we recommend watching for.

### **Focusing on Clicks Instead of Leads**

A campaign can generate traffic without producing meaningful inquiries. Measure form submissions, calls, appointments, and qualified leads instead of treating every click as a successful outcome.

### **Using the Same Message for Every Audience**

A person searching for an emergency service has different needs from someone casually browsing social media. Your advertising message should reflect the audience's level of interest.

### **Sending Visitors to an Unrelated Landing Page**

The landing page should match the ad's promise. If your advertisement promotes a specific service, make it easy for visitors to find information about that service and contact your business.

### **Ignoring Conversion Tracking**

Without accurate tracking, it is difficult to understand which campaigns are generating valuable actions. Configure the appropriate conversion events and test them regularly.

### **Responding Too Slowly to Inquiries**

A lead can lose interest if nobody responds. Establish a process for contacting new inquiries, recording their status, and following up when necessary.

For additional guidance, Google provides information on[ **conversion measurement**](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722022), while Meta's[ **lead generation guide**](https://about.fb.com/ltam/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2023/11/LeadGenerationGuide.pdf) discusses lead forms, messaging, and connecting lead information with customer management systems.
